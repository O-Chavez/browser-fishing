### Bugs and tooling

- `npm run smoke` only runs on a Mac as is: `CHROME_PATH` defaults to the macOS Chrome and it launches with `--use-angle=metal`. On Linux (cloud sessions) it needs a wrapper that adds `--no-sandbox` and SwiftShader, and even then slow software frames fail ~8 timing checks (townsfolk walks, reel fights, roulette pacing) and the offline checks time out. A Linux-friendly launch and longer timeouts there would let cloud sessions run it.
- The bench scenes have no high-tier fish, so they can't measure the fish tier effects

### Performance

- The Wardrobe preview still compiles a legend piece's shaders the first time it's tried on (from the deep sea cosmetics step 2 stutter fix, 2026-11-14): the preview has its own WebGL context (`CharacterPreview`, `src/game/character.ts`), so the world's warm-up doesn't cover it; about 4 programs a piece, not measured. It could warm its own shaders when the Wardrobe or Reed's tab opens
- On a slow device the legend pieces' warm-up (`Avatars.warmLegends`) finishes a few seconds after the map's own (SwiftShader: about 3 s later, then about 2.9 s of draw-time shader work), so a player who joins within about 6 s of the map being ready gets that pause in game instead of on the join screen. Real phones compile in the background, so this is likely pessimistic; if it shows up, build the stand-ins before the map's warm-up so the join screen's hold covers them (about 65 ms more at startup on desktop, 250 ms on a slow CPU)
- The harbour town costs a little more than the old one (A/B bench against the pre-rework main, 2026-11-10): the town views render ~9% slower (2.7 -> 3.0 ms on an M1, day and night) and the lakeside map builds ~50 ms slower (163 -> 216 ms). Both came with the walkthrough pass: four more windows per house, garden fences, town trees, and grass tufts on the town pad. Profile the town build and the tuft count in town, and claw it back
- 🎨 Fewer real point lights: cutting them from 8 to 4 was ~20% faster but visibly darkened night streets and the casino ceiling. Outdoors, the night glow pools (`src/game/nightglow.ts`) now stand in for far lamps, so a lower cap might look fine there; the casino chandeliers would need pools of their own
- The deep sea art pass's cost (A/B bench, 2026-11-10): sea scenes render ~50% slower than before it (about 1.4 to 2.1 ms a frame on an M1, 6-7 more draws: the boat's detail, the horizon cloud, rain, gulls, the buoy and a heavier sea shader), the first charter's first frame takes ~25 ms longer (new shaders to compile; could join the prewarm, `src/game/prewarm.ts`), and the pond is ~3% slower from the shared moon, stars and sky grain. Still cheaper than the pond, so left as is; look here first if weak phones struggle at sea
- Building the pond map blocks for ~80 ms when travelling there; could be cached or split across frames
- Shadows cost 3-6 ms per frame at the old resolution; fewer shadow casters/receivers on small props could help weaker GPUs
- The first click or touch stalls about 200 ms in headless Chrome while `audio.start()` makes the `AudioContext` (`AudioOutputDevice::GetOutputDeviceInfo`, a synchronous call). It could be made at load (it starts suspended) and only resumed in the click, if real devices show the same stall
- Shader programs that still compile on first use, because their objects aren't in the scene until later: fish models (`buildFish`, first catch or another player holding one), held items when switching hotbar slots, drops, and the join screen's character preview (its own WebGL context, ~1 s on SwiftShader before the notice is even dismissed). Hidden stand-ins in the scene, like the one `Avatars` keeps for `World.prewarm`, would cover the in-world ones
- 🎨 The load-hitching fix moved all shader compiling behind the join screen, so on a slow phone the join screen freezes once for longer (SwiftShader: ~3 s of compiling became ~6 s there, and none after joining). If that's noticeable on real phones, the compile could be spread over several frames, or the warm-up draw skipped where the browser compiles fully at link time
- Returning players don't get the full load-hitching fix: their join card hides as soon as the server answers the auto-join (`fishing-auto`, `Game.finishJoin`), often before the background `compileAsync` (`World.prewarm`) is done, and `holdForShaders` only holds while `#join` is up, so the first frames in game still wait on the shaders left. No worse than before, and `warmDraw` still covers what's out of view. Measure with `npm run bench:load` on an auto-join, then e.g. keep the join card up ("Loading…") until the prewarm finishes
