# A · Ember Point (round 3: passed 5 of 5 in round 2, polish notes applied)

Plan: `plan-a.jpg` (lake at the top, grid 4 m). Coordinates are metres on the map (x east, z toward town). Earlier
versions are in `round1/` and `round2/`.

**Polish since the round 2 vote** (all from the judges' notes, nothing else changed): both headland pines stand
behind the fence, so the Totem is alone against the water; the cove seat wall is unbroken, with its lantern on a stone
pier, and the crown has two more posts on that side; a clear 2 m way runs from the beach steps to the jetty with the
beach's logs east of it and the Totem on the tip west of it; the crown hangs from the arch's post at the gate; a
flower bed fills the wedge behind Thank You; a plaque at Bram's counter says "Light it on the jetty"; the open floor
on the cove side stays clear of props.

**Changed after the build (Oscar, 2026-11-17):** the Derby stand is out of this release and comes back when the Weekly
Derby is built. Its bay holds a flower bed like the one behind Thank You. Where the text below names the Derby stand,
read that bed.

**The idea.** The camp is a small open-air theatre on the east headland, facing the cove. One round terrace holds the
fire and its seats; its back is a wall of five panels (Thank You, Lake Legends, the host's stall, Deep Sea, the Derby
stand); the cove side is a stone-faced rim. It is the cove's beacon: every player on the pier, the jetties, the quay
and Reed's wharf looks straight at it.

**Site and size.** The headland where the small campfire is today. A cobbled terrace 11 m across at (20.5, 2.7), a
pebble beach on the point's lake side and a jetty off it. About 15 m by 21 m in all. The ridge behind is cut away up
to a metre under the terrace's back half and held by a stone retaining wall.

**Arriving.** The campfire lane from the quay's east end is unchanged. Where the lanes meet (17.4, 9.4) a signpost
has three fingers, Fire, Stones and Rockets, and three steps on you pass under a timber arch, ANGLERS' REST, with a
lantern on each post, onto the terrace.

**The centrepiece.** The Great Fire: a stone hearth 2.1 m across with flames to head height, about twice today's fire.
Above it a crown of lights: one ring of bulbs strung round ten tall posts on the rim and the arch's post. At night the crown and the fire
are one lit ring over the water. The terrace's cove face is stone, the same as the retaining wall, so from the pier
it reads as a plinth with the fire on it.

**Measures of the circle.** Hearth, then six log seats for two at 2.9 m, then a clear ring 1.8 m wide all the way
round behind the logs, then the rim. Walkers, buyers and board readers use the ring; nobody has to pass between a
sitter and the fire. The log ring has 2 m openings on the gate's line and the beach steps' line.

**Seats (23 at the fire).** The six logs (12), each with its instrument standing by it (banjo, drum, squeezebox,
whistle, washboard, jug): sit there and you play it. A low stone seat wall along the cove-side rim (11), which is also
that side's cordon, unbroken, with a lantern on a stone pier at its middle. The cobbles between the hearth and that
wall are kept clear: the one open floor where a group can stand together with the cove behind.

**The wall of panels.** Round the back of the rim, at floor level, each in its own bay with no seat in front of it:
Thank You; Lake Legends; Bram's Kettle; Deep Sea; the Derby stand. They are read from the clear ring. Each catch
board carries its record fish, mounted above it, with the holder's name on a plaque big enough to read from the logs.
Bram's Kettle is the host's stall, a pale blue clapboard shack 3.2 m wide in the family of Marlow's, with a striped
awning, a kettle on the counter and a crate of rockets; it is set a metre back into the cut so its counter has its own
standing apron off the ring. Bram Tinder, the fire keeper, sells three rockets: a small one, a big one and a finale
priced for the richest anglers (the prices are Oscar's). A plaque at the counter says "Light it on the jetty". Behind the panels: the retaining wall, a hedge on its top and
the town's white picket fence, which runs on to the gate and down to the beach.

**Skipper's Beach.** Two-metre-wide steps down from the terrace's lake side onto a pebble beach, about 7 m by 5 m,
facing the open lake. The stone pile and the skip sign are at its east end; two log seats and a bench stand 3 m and
more from the pile, east of a clear 2 m way from the steps to the jetty. The Legends' Totem stands alone on the
beach's west tip, west of the jetty's foot, where the pier sees it against the water: carved fish stacked 3 m high, the lake's legends below, the sea's above, the
Kraken on top.

**The rocket jetty.** A timber jetty 2.2 m by 5.5 m from the beach out over the open lake, with the rocket rack and a
lantern at its end, 9 m from the nearest seat at the fire and 5 m from the beach's logs. Rockets go up over open water in view of the terrace, the
pier and the quay. You can cast from it, but like Reed's wharf it fishes as the shore does.

**Prompts.** Seats, boards and the stall each ask the player to face them (the rule the casino's exit already uses),
so standing in the ring facing a board gives the board, and facing a log gives "Sit down". The stones, the rocket
rack, the counter and the nearest seats are each 3 m or more apart.

**Night.** Lights that cast: the fire and the stall's lantern. Everything else glows.

**What moves.** Both headland pines move a few metres, to stand behind the fence at the north-east. Nothing else
in town moves. Bottle "Warm hands by the fire, cold water at my feet" ends up on the beach.

**Building it.** One round level, one cut with a wall, one dock rectangle. New props: the hearth, the stall (Marlow's
shack re-proportioned), totem, rocket rack, hedge, flat stones, plaques. No forest tree is touched.

**Risks.** The terrace reaches about a metre further into the cove than today's ring. It is a place you go to, at the
end of its lane, not one you pass through.
