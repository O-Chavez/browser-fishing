# B · Tarn Camp

Plan: `plan-b.jpg` (lake at the top, grid 4 m). Coordinates are metres on the map (x east, z toward town).

**The idea.** A sheltered camp behind the headland's ridge, beside its own small pond, the tarn. You reach it through
a gate and a walled cut in the ridge, and it opens out round the fire with water beyond. The cove keeps a beacon; the
camp itself is the hidden, cozy place behind it.

**Site and size.** From the cove's east shore to the tarn's far bank: about 23 m by 17 m. The fire hollow is a cobbled
circle 8.4 m across at (27.6, 2.4), cut up to 1.2 m into the back of the ridge, whose bank is held by a stone wall
round the hollow's west and north. The tarn (a natural puddle today) is dug to about 5 m by 7.5 m of water.

**Arriving, from the cove.** The lane from the quay passes a signpost and goes under the ANGLERS' REST arch onto the
Landing, a small cobbled round at the water where today's fire is. On it: a beacon brazier on a stone plinth, the
Legends' Totem on its rim, one bench. From there the Cut leads east: a 2.2 m cobbled way between two low stone walls
with four lanterns. The Cut is sighted on the pier: standing where players spawn, you look across the cove, past the
beacon, through the Cut, at the Great Fire.

**Arriving, by the back lane.** A second cobbled lane leaves the same meeting of lanes and comes round the ridge's
foot, past Thank You and the Derby stand, in at the fire's south side. So the camp is a loop, not a dead end.

**The centrepiece.** The Great Fire, a stone hearth 2.1 m across, under a crown of lights strung round six tall posts.
The tarn lies right behind it, so the fire is seen with its reflection.

**Seats (26).** Eight log seats for two in a full circle round the hearth, each with its instrument. The stone seat
wall in two arcs along the ridge side (10 seats), either side of the Cut's mouth. Deep Sea and Lake Legends stand on
the bank above the arcs, each with its record fish mounted over it, facing the fire and the water.

**The tarn.** A pebble beach along its near shore, a step from the cobbles: the stone pile and the skip sign. Out on
the water a moored raft carries the rocket rack; the fuse post stands on the beach. Rockets go up from the middle of
the pond in front of everyone at the fire, 8 m from the nearest seat, and show over the trees from town. A rowboat is
moored at the south end. The far bank is the hillside: rocks, reeds and the forest.

**Bram's Kettle.** The host's stall stands at the fire's south-east, under the big leafy tree that already grows
there, with a lit lantern: a butter-yellow shack with a striped awning, a patch of lawn and the back lane beside it.

**Edges.** The ridge itself is the west and north wall, with a hedge and three pines planted along its top. A white
picket fence runs along the back lane and round the stall's lawn to the tarn. The tarn and the hillside close the
east. The only ways in are the Cut and the back lane.

**Night.** Lights that cast: the Great Fire and the stall's lantern. The beacon, the crown and the Cut's lanterns glow.

**What moves.** The small fire becomes the Landing's beacon and the boards move into the hollow. The forest tree by
the stall and the one on the tarn's south bank stay.

**Building it.** One round level cut into the ridge with a wall, a pond, a short walled passage, a lawn. New props:
hearth, brazier, stall, totem, raft, hedge, flat stones. This is the most earthwork of the three.

**Risks.** The fire is behind the ridge: from the quay you see its glow and crown, not its seats. It is 9 m further
from the quay than today's fire.
